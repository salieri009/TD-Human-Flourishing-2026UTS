# Speaker Notes & Citations Per Slide

## Slide 1 — Title
**"Shaping Technologies That Shape Us"**

Speaker note: Open with the paradox. "Every tool shapes its user. A hammer builds muscle. A calculator builds dependency. What does Copilot build?"

---

## Slide 2 — AI Is Inevitable
Speaker note: Don't argue against AI. We accept it's here. The question is: how do we design it so it shapes students *toward* capability, not away from it?

---

## Slide 3 — Context
Speaker note: Establish who's in the room. University students. Daily AI users. Stakes: their cognitive development over 3–4 years.

---

## Slide 4 — Shift to Cognitive Offloading
**Citation to display on slide:**
> Fan et al. (2025): 47% of students delegate tasks directly to AI. The tasks most delegated are *Creating* (39.8%) and *Analysing* (30.2%) — the highest rungs of Bloom's Taxonomy.

Speaker note: This isn't about using Grammarly to fix commas. Students are handing over the *thinking itself*. When AI does the analysis, who learns to analyse?

---

## Slide 5 — Impact on Human Flourishing
**Citation to display on slide:**
> Fan et al. (2025): RCT, n=117. AI-assisted essays scored significantly higher (Cohen's d=0.7). Knowledge retention: no difference.

Speaker note: Better essays. No learning. That's the Performance Paradox. The artefact improves. The person doesn't.

---

## Slide 4.5 — Human Story (NEW — insert between 4 and 5)
**No citation needed — narrative slide**

Speaker note: Make it personal. Slow down here. Let "Yuna" land before moving to data.

> Meet Yuna. Second-year IT student. Never misses a deadline. Her Copilot history is 200 sessions deep. Last week her tutor asked her to walk through her reasoning on a data model she submitted. She couldn't. She'd never built the reasoning — Copilot had.
>
> Yuna isn't failing. She's hollow.

---

## Slide 6 — Designing AI (Divider)
Speaker note: Pivot. "So what do we actually do?" This isn't a critique session — we're here with a solution.

---

## Slide 7 — Interrogative Prompts
**Citation to display on slide:**
> Singh, Guan & Rieh (2025), n=40: Metacognitive prompts → significantly more topics explored (p=0.006), greater persistent inquiry (p=0.047).
> Exintaris et al. (2023), n=204: Using AI output as a critique prompt improved metacognitive engagement.

Speaker note: The difference between an AI that answers and an AI that asks. "What do you already know?" is a 5-word design decision that changes what students learn from an interaction.

---

## Slide 8 — Learning vs Answer Mode
**Citation to display on slide:**
> Marín Díaz (2025), n=1,273: Three cognitive profiles emerge from AI interaction patterns — Dependent, Moderate, Critical-Autonomous.

Speaker note: Show the toggle. This is what we're proposing. Two modes, one switch. Microsoft's Education tenant defaults to Learning Mode. Students who need answer access can switch — but the default shapes behaviour.

---

## Slide 9 — Self-Reflection
**Citation to display on slide:**
> Singh et al. (2025): Consolidation prompts ("Summarise what you learned") significantly improved knowledge synthesis.

Speaker note: The session log isn't surveillance — it's a mirror. Students see what they attempted, how many hints they needed, what they figured out on their own. That's metacognitive data they currently have no access to.

---

## Slide 10 — Limitations
**Citation to display on slide:**
> Castillo (2024): AI does not significantly enhance critical thinking vs. traditional methods in controlled studies.

Speaker note: We're not claiming this will fix everything. Sample sizes are small. Self-report bias is real. But the direction of the evidence is clear, and the cost of designing this right is low.

---

## Slide 10.5 — Call to Action (NEW — insert before Thank You)
**No citation needed**

Speaker note: Direct address to the room. "You're Microsoft. You have 345 million Office users. A toggle in Copilot for Education reaches every university on your tenant. That's not a research study — that's a policy change."

> Microsoft, you built tools that billions trust.  
> Now build tools that make them think.
>
> **Four asks:**
> 1. Learning Mode / Answer Mode toggle — default to Learning in edu tenants
> 2. Interrogative prompting — AI asks before it answers
> 3. Session reflection logging — make the interaction visible to the learner
> 4. Cognitive profile adaptation — calibrate scaffolding to user pattern

---

## Slide 11 — Thank You
Speaker note: Leave the room on the core message: "AI that makes students better thinkers is better AI. That's good for learners, good for institutions, and good for Microsoft."
